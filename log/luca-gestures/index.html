Movement needs interpretation.

The augmented-violin archive brings hand sensing, pitch following, and synthesis into one performance setup. Two BNO055 sensors send orientation, acceleration, gravity, and calibration data through a Teensy to Max. The useful design work happens between those measurements and a musical control.

Keep calibration in the data

Each sensor contributes 17 values, including four calibration indicators. The firmware sends both sensors in a single serial record. The Max module separates the left and right streams before normalization and smoothing.

Separate motion from confidence

Orientation and pitch confidence answer different questions. The sine module has distinct controls for external phase, amplitude source, and pitch capture. Its notes identify small differences between oscillators as a way to produce beating.

Preserve a stable sound

The confidence gate holds a previous pitch when tracking becomes uncertain. The module also offers input amplitude, pitch confidence, or a manual slider as amplitude sources. Those choices let a performer decide how closely the synthesized sound follows the acoustic instrument.

Original implementation and scope

Augmented-violin archive, 2023. Sources: two_BNOs.ino, submodule_BNO055data.maxpat, and module_sines.maxpat. Sensor access uses Adafruit's BNO055 library. The final patch-to-performance correspondence is not established, so the sensor and pitch examples are presented separately. Linear angle normalization also preserves the original 360°/0° boundary; this demo does not imply that wraparound was solved.
