Three ways of making atmosphere

Colour is a behaviour.

Compare the structures before choosing the hues. Every direction uses the same projects and the same black ink. Switch between them at any point in the long page.

01

Atmosphere through duration

Tidal register

A single field moves slowly between mineral blue, green-grey and chalk. The page feels continuous; a narrow warm interval changes the temperature of everything around it.

Air#c5d7dbWater#a8bfbbChalk#e5e6d9Depth#344e56Ember#bb593e
01Blue air02Green-grey03Clear light04Deep water05Chalk

What changes

The ground changes with scroll. A pale sheet moves only a little.

What it offers

The most continuous reading experience. Photographs and text share one atmosphere.

Watch for

If the range becomes too wide, the whole page can feel like a colour animation. Long holds matter.

02

Atmosphere through adjacency

Cut planes

Large, unequal areas of blue, celadon and grey meet at precise edges. A third opaque colour suggests an overlap. Space comes from proportion and comparison.

Blue#9fbac9Celadon#becbbbOverlap#879f9eSlate#3c515bOxide#bb593e
01Blue / celadon02Grey / blue03Paper / green04Slate / mist05Warm grey

What changes

Opaque planes replace one another. Their edges stay visible.

What it offers

The clearest graphic structure. Strong separation between projects without enclosing them in cards.

Watch for

Equal-sized areas quickly become a patchwork. Keep one field dominant and the others subordinate.

03

Atmosphere through material

Suspended sheets

A cooler, denser ground is covered by thin pale sheets. Some lines sit underneath; annotations sit above. Offset edges and partial coverage make the depth legible.

Ground#819ca7Milk#e5e9deGreen film#afc8bdNight#304951Light#d6a551
01Cool ground02Milk-blue sheet03Green film04Night / vellum05Uncovered paper

What changes

Translucent sheets cross drawings and the edges of photographs.

What it offers

The strongest connection to drawing, paper membranes and physical construction.

Watch for

Keep labels above the sheet. Cover only part of a photograph so its unfiltered colour remains available.

Two additional ideas held open

Borrowed light

Let the margins around a photograph inherit a small part of its temperature. This might connect image and field, but automatic image sampling risks making every project feel unrelated. A later test could use a few hand-chosen, subdued surrounds.

Density as colour

Keep the ground almost constant and let closely spaced black lines darken it. Orr Marshall's line-spacing study suggests a useful fourth variable. The present prototypes touch on it; a fuller version should use the actual generative systems.
