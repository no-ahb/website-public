Research notebook / 24 September 2026

Turner, through Albers.

Turner supplies relationships between light, colour and area. Albers supplies a way to test them. The experiments translate those relationships into broad surfaces, precise edges and thin layers.

These are selective working references. The observations of Turner's three works, the Salubra keyboard and the Albers student exercises refer to digital reproductions inspected during research. Colour strips below are analytical sketches, not sampled or colour-calibrated reproductions.

Albers's exercises are supported by the Foundation's teaching records. Yale's digital edition is linked for further exploration; its interactive reader was unavailable in this browser session. Each proposed application is a design hypothesis to test in the portfolio.

Turner / relationships before depiction

01.1

J. M. W. Turner

Sea View ↗

Mid-1820s
Watercolour and bodycolour on blue paper

A narrow, dark green-blue sea sits under a much larger sky. Blue-violet cloud gathers on the left; a pale opening cuts diagonally through it. Gold sails and small apricot passages are warmer and more concentrated than the grey-green water.

Principle to borrow

Give the cool field most of the area. Let a small warm mark gain intensity from its surroundings, and let one pale opening interrupt a denser field.

Where to test it

The same rust fragment appears on two cool grounds in the laboratory. Suspended sheets also treats the coloured ground as part of the image.

Primary source ↗Vaughan Bequest collection ↗
01.2

J. M. W. Turner

A beginning ↗

c.1840s, as dated in the NGA exhibition
Watercolour on paper; Tate / NGA exhibition record 230451

This particular sheet is predominantly grey and olive, with a thin, pale yellow-green band near its middle and a small dark cluster just left of centre. The lightest passage has little saturation. Most of the paper stays close in value.

Principle to borrow

Luminosity can come from a narrow change in value and temperature. A luminous interval need not be bright orange, white, or highly saturated.

Where to test it

Tidal register holds muted fields for long stretches, then opens into chalk. The line drawings provide the few dense dark marks.

Primary source ↗NGA: Three seascapes ↗
01.3

J. M. W. Turner

Three seascapes ↗

c.1827
Oil on canvas; Tate, exhibited at the National Gallery of Australia

A dark violet-brown cap, a wide warm-grey field, a narrow blue-grey seam and a paler lower field make a striking vertical sequence. NGA explains that Turner turned the support around so one sky could serve two marine subjects.

Principle to borrow

A field can belong to two adjacent compositions. The boundary between projects can share a colour instead of terminating at a white gutter.

Where to test it

Tidal register carries a ground across content boundaries. Cut planes tests the opposite: an explicit seam changes how the same layout is read.

Primary source ↗NGA: Turner and the sea ↗

Albers / the surrounding colour

02.1

Josef Albers

Interaction of Color: one colour becomes two ↗

1963; Foundation workshop and digital edition
Colour exercise

The exercise holds the small colour constant and changes its surround. Its apparent brightness and temperature are relational; an RGB value alone does not specify the experience.

Principle to borrow

Evaluate an accent in at least two real contexts. Keep the patch identical, move it between blue-grey and green-grey, then isolate it on one neutral ground.

Where to test it

The laboratory has paired identical warm fragments and an isolation control. All three studies keep the decorative ink at #17211f.

Primary source ↗Interaction of Color, Yale digital edition ↗Foundation teaching resources ↗
02.2

Livingston, student of Josef Albers

Color study: make one color look like two ↗

c.1951–58, Yale University
Color-aid paper on cardboard

Two narrow purple bars occupy adjacent light-lavender and deep-violet grounds. The left bar reads darker; the right reads lighter. A small joining patch at the bottom helps expose the relationship.

Principle to borrow

Keep the comparison simple enough to inspect. A repeated fragment is more useful than two entire competing palettes.

Where to test it

The paired warm fragments and grey isolation view make that comparison explicit. Violet also remains an open possibility beyond the first three blue-green studies.

Primary source ↗Foundation: One Color Becomes Two ↗
02.3

Roland Peagle, student of Josef Albers

Color study: transparency / film color ↗

c.1951–58, Yale University
Color-aid paper mounted on cardboard

A pale blue-green rectangle sits across a folded yellow form. Olive intersections and cooler, muted fragments make the planes seem to pass through one another. The small value steps matter more than a dramatic difference in hue. The Foundation identifies the material as Color-aid paper.

Principle to borrow

Give an overlap its own solid colour. Compare that constructed illusion with an actual translucent sheet over the same forms.

Where to test it

Cut planes uses a deliberately darker opaque overlap. The laboratory switches between a chosen solid mixture and real alpha transparency.

Primary source ↗The Logic and Magic of Color: mixing and transparence ↗Interaction of Color ↗

Space / surface / instrument

03.1

Le Corbusier / Salubra

Colour keyboards and framing cards ↗

1931 and 1959
Architectural colour-selection tools

Small colours sit within much larger background areas. A cut-out card isolates combinations; the same samples can be compared against different grounds. The surrounding warm neutrals are as consequential as the stronger blues and greens.

Principle to borrow

Treat a section as a coloured surface with an area and an edge. Test its spatial effect in context. Use a neutral viewing mask to separate a colour from its neighbours.

Where to test it

Cut planes uses unequal areas. The laboratory isolation control and the comparison page borrow the viewing procedure, not the historical colour codes.

03.2

Teenage Engineering

PO modular series ↗

Product reference
Painted structures, black controls and printed labels

Colour occupies the structural surface; black knobs, sockets and labels remain clear against it. On the yellow and red instruments, colour makes a physical assembly immediately readable.

Principle to borrow

Concentrate warmth at a point of interaction. Keep the marks and lettering crisp, and make the coloured area large enough to feel like a material part.

Where to test it

The recording button is a small warm surface within the cool instrument field. The palette controls offer ochre, oxide, orange and red without changing the whole page.

Drawing / information / unexpected connections

04.1

Orr Marshall, student of Josef Albers

Study in varied distances between lines of identical weights ↗

Yale University; date not specified in the record
Pencil on paper

Closely spaced horizontal lines gather into darker vertical bands on the pale sheet. Changing intervals make some areas seem denser without changing pencil weight. Density itself becomes a tonal field.

Principle to borrow

Keep the ink and stroke weight constant. Change spacing, background value and sheet coverage to see whether the drawing reads as a surface, a structure or a distant trace.

Where to test it

The same generated line study recurs at different densities and on different grounds. On the deepest field, it is deliberately low contrast until a sheet passes over it.

Primary source ↗Albers: teaching through direct observation ↗
04.2

Giorgia Lupi and Stefanie Posavec

Dear Data ↗

2014–15
Weekly hand-drawn data postcards

Small marks carry specific meanings, with detailed keys explaining how to read them. The drawings have a personal rhythm while remaining interpretable.

Principle to borrow

Give meaningful marks a stable key and a quiet area to sit. Let the atmosphere surround the information rather than requiring every colour to encode something.

Where to test it

Recording axes and labels remain legible. The new decorative drawings are explicitly labelled as generated studies, so they cannot be mistaken for measured project data.

Primary source ↗Giorgia Lupi: publications ↗Giorgia Lupi: work ↗
04.3

Stamen

Minimalist global basemaps for Notion.ai ↗

2025
Light and dark cartographic systems

Stamen describes a restrained base map designed to allow markers to stand out, with separate light and dark versions. The ground supplies orientation while remaining subordinate to the foreground task.

Principle to borrow

Separate atmosphere from information hierarchy. Preserve labels on stable light surfaces when the surrounding field becomes deep or dense.

Where to test it

The deepest section retains black drawings but puts readable copy on a pale sheet. The live instrument has a light support even when the wider page darkens.

Primary source ↗Stamen: work ↗

Working rules, open to revision

Composition before palette.

Start with one dominant cool field, a second quieter surface and one concentrated warm mark. The warm fragments are small by composition, rather than governed by a claimed percentage from a painting.

Keep a stable reading surface. The atmospheric ground can deepen around it. Important labels and controls should remain readable; low contrast belongs only to decorative marks.

Edges carry the material.

Use a change in colour, a small offset and partial coverage to suggest a thin sheet. A sheet can mute a drawing physically through alpha, or suggest transparency through a chosen opaque intersection. These are different mechanisms and deserve separate tests.

Scroll is the input. Nothing cycles while you stand still. Reduced-motion settings hold a composed static version of each direction.

Gaps & next questions

Test these relationships on different displays, with your own drawings, and over longer stretches of project copy. Screen reproductions cannot establish Turner's original pigment colour. The atmospheric effects here remain proposals; no universal claim is made that a particular hue always advances, recedes or feels calm.

The most promising unexpected connection is Orr Marshall's study: line density may become a fourth way to change a field. The next comparison could also test muted violet and earth grey, both present in the Turner studies, against the narrower blue-green range.
