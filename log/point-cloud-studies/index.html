An image can become a space.

In Suspended Violin, the instrument becomes a point model that a camera can enter. A separate group of GAN tests explores what happens when a recorded face is reconstructed as a generated image. These are two different processes, each with its own visible artifacts.

Select an original camera view, then enlarge the render. These are archived images, not a live 3D reconstruction.
  1. Physical subjectThe violin and its suspended cables remain visible in the source photograph.
  2. Point representationSurface detail breaks into separate samples and gaps.
  3. Camera compositionChanging viewpoint makes the object legible, fragmented, or interior.
  4. Audiovisual pieceRendered movement accompanies violin composed in fifth-order ambisonics.

Keep the gaps visible

The preserved renders retain the point structure. At a distance, the scroll and cables describe an object; close up, empty areas and scattered samples become part of the image. A solid surface would hide that behavior.

Compose the viewpoint

The camera is an expressive control. The three views above compare scale and position within the same project. They are not registered before-and-after images, and the enlargement control does not invent additional detail.

Keep intermediate material

The archive includes the subject photograph, a Blender scene, presentation stills, and rendered frame sequences. Those stages make the transformation inspectable even without loading the original, much larger point model.

A second kind of reconstruction.

The GAN experiments begin with a filmed face and preserve both sampled source frames and generated exports. The comparison exposes changes in facial shape and background continuity. It belongs beside the point-cloud work as another experiment in representation.

Eight seconds from the saved output. This is a recorded model experiment; there is no inference running in this player.

Original artifacts and scope

Suspended Violin, 2021, was presented at Stanford CCRMA. The artist documentation describes a 300-million-point model. The web comparison uses original renders from the presentation archive; the raw point data and reconstruction settings have not been recovered here. Watch the completed audiovisual work.

The GAN comparison comes from the feeling space / GAN tests / self test 2 folder. Its source recording, sampled frames, and output are preserved together. The export does not identify a model checkpoint or training procedure, so neither is claimed. The point-cloud and GAN examples should not be read as one continuous pipeline.
